Constitutional Law

Fundamental legal framework governing Bulgaria's state structure and citizen rights

Constitution of Bulgaria

Adopted: 1991 Last Updated: 2023

The supreme law of Bulgaria, establishing the structure of government, the rights of citizens, and the fundamental principles of the state.

Overview

The Constitution of Bulgaria is the highest normative act that prescribes the order of organization and activity of the state and defines the fundamental rights and obligations of its citizens. It was adopted following the fall of communism and has been amended several times to reflect evolving democratic principles.

Key Provisions
  • Republic Form: Bulgaria is established as a parliamentary republic
  • Democratic Values: Protection of human rights, rule of law, and democratic governance
  • Separation of Powers: Division between legislative, executive, and judicial branches
  • Fundamental Rights: Rights to life, liberty, privacy, freedom of expression and association
  • Citizenship: Rules governing Bulgarian nationality and citizenship rights
Amendments

The Constitution has been amended in 1997, 2015, and 2023 to strengthen democratic institutions, improve judicial independence, and address contemporary legal issues.

Law on Parliament

Adopted: 1990 Last Updated: 2022

Regulates the organization and functioning of the National Assembly, Bulgaria's unicameral legislative body.

Overview

This law governs the structure, composition, and operations of the National Assembly of the Republic of Bulgaria, which is the highest body of state authority. It establishes the rights and responsibilities of members of parliament and procedures for legislative processes.

Main Aspects
  • Composition: 240 members elected for a 4-year term
  • Committees: Organization of parliamentary committees and their functions
  • Procedures: Rules for parliamentary sessions, voting, and legislation
  • Immunities: Parliamentary immunity and privileges of members
  • Oversight: Parliamentary control over government and state institutions

Law on the President

Adopted: 1991 Last Updated: 2023

Establishes the powers, duties, and procedures related to the office of the President of Bulgaria.

Overview

This law defines the role of the President as head of state, including powers in defense, foreign policy, and judicial matters. The President is elected for a five-year term and serves as the chief representative of Bulgaria in international relations.

Key Provisions
  • Election: Direct popular election for a 5-year term
  • Commander-in-Chief: Powers over the armed forces
  • Pardons: Authority to grant pardons and amnesty
  • State Decorations: Authority to award state honors
  • International Relations: Representation in foreign affairs
