Criminal harassment and uttering threats guidance

Many people feel shocked after police lay charges for harassment or uttering threats. Words, messages, or conduct often form the basis of these allegations. Courts treat such charges as serious matters. A conviction brings lasting consequences, including jail time and a criminal record.

BG Laws provides focused criminal defence for clients facing harassment or uttering threats allegations. Strong preparation and early action matter.

What Counts as Criminal Harassment

Criminal harassment includes patterns of conduct that cause fear for safety. Common examples include:

  • Repeated phone calls, text messages, or emails.
  • Showing up at a person’s home, workplace, or school.
  • Following someone in public places.
  • Monitoring or watching someone without consent.

A single incident sometimes supports a charge when conduct creates serious fear.

What Counts as Uttering Threats

Uttering threats involves statements expressing an intention to cause harm. Allegations often involve:

  • Threats of physical harm to a person.
  • Threats of damage to property.
  • Statements implying violence, even without direct wording.

Context, tone, and surrounding circumstances play a central role in court analysis.

Domestic and Non-Domestic Situations

Police frequently lay these charges in domestic settings. Spouses, partners, or former partners often face allegations after emotional disputes. Charges also arise between coworkers, business partners, neighbors, or strangers in public places.

Relationship type does not reduce risk. Courts treat each allegation seriously.

Penalties Upon Conviction

  • Uttering threats carries exposure up to five years of jail.
  • Criminal harassment carries exposure up to ten years of jail.
  • Bail conditions often restrict contact, movement, and employment.
  • A conviction affects immigration status, travel, and employment.

Early legal strategy protects rights from the first court date.
