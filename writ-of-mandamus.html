Writ of mandamus guidance

A writ of mandamus is a legal remedy that compels a government body or official to perform a duty that the law requires. In immigration law, this means asking the Federal Court of Canada to force a decision-maker to act when an application has been unreasonably delayed or when the responsible authority has failed to fulfil a clear legal obligation.

When It Applies

You might consider a writ of mandamus when:

  • Your immigration application, temporary or permanent, has been pending far beyond normal processing times without explanation.
  • You have completed all required steps, fulfilled your obligations and there is no active internal appeal or mechanism forcing action.
  • No other effective remedy exists to move the process forward.
  • The delay or refusal is not due to an exercise of genuine discretion but reflects inaction or unreasonable delay.

The Process

  • First you or your lawyer send a well-crafted demand letter to the immigration authority, requesting action within a set period.
  • If the duty remains unfulfilled, you file for an Application for Leave to the Federal Court, asking permission to proceed with the writ.
  • The Court may grant leave and schedule a hearing or deal with the matter on the papers.
  • At the hearing you must show that the parameters of the test are met and argue your case; the government will respond with its reasons for delay or inaction.
  • If successful, the Court issues the writ ordering the authority to act by a certain date. The underlying application is then processed without further delay, but success of the original immigration matter is not guaranteed.

Why Use This Remedy

  • It provides a direct legal tool to compel action when other options are exhausted.
  • The presence of a filed writ often motivates the immigration authority to act quickly to avoid court imposition.
  • It protects applicants whose lives, families or business interests depend on urgent resolution of their immigration file.
