Fraud charges threaten freedom, finances, and reputation. Courts treat financial crime seriously. A conviction leads to jail time, fines, restitution orders, and a lasting criminal record. Early legal strategy matters.

What Fraud Means

Fraud occurs when someone uses deceit, falsehoods, or dishonest conduct to obtain property, money, services, or advantage. To secure a conviction, the Crown must prove beyond reasonable doubt:

  • Dishonest act or false representation.
  • Intent to deceive or defraud.
  • Resulting deprivation or risk of loss to another person.

Intent distinguishes fraud from mistakes or misunderstanding.

Common Fraud Allegations

Fraud charges arise in many situations, including:

  • Credit card or identity misuse.
  • Insurance or benefits fraud.
  • Mortgage or loan application fraud.
  • Investment or securities fraud.
  • Employment related fraud.
  • False billing or expense claims.

Each case turns on facts, evidence, and intentions.

Penalties Upon Conviction

Fraud carries serious consequences. Penalties vary with value and context:

  • Indictable offence exposure to years in prison.
  • Large scale or high value fraud often attracts higher sentences.
  • Restitution or compensation orders may apply.
  • Fines and probation are common.

A conviction affects employment, professional licensing, and travel privileges.

Key Defence Issues

Fraud cases often hinge on complex evidence. Key defence considerations include:

  • Was there dishonest intent or an honest mistake?
  • Did the Crown prove the misrepresentation or concealment?
  • Are records, documents, or digital evidence reliable?
  • Are there errors in identification or witness credibility?
  • Were rights protected during investigation and arrest?
