Impaired driving and DUI defence guidance

Driving while impaired by alcohol, drugs or a combination of both is a serious criminal offence in Canada. Even a small amount of impairment can trigger charges, and the consequences extend far beyond a fine, affecting your freedom, record, immigration status and professional future.

What the Law Covers

Impaired driving includes:

  • Operating a motor vehicle while your ability is impaired by alcohol or drugs.
  • Having a blood alcohol concentration of 80 milligrams or more of alcohol per 100 millilitres of blood within two hours of driving.
  • Having prohibited levels of certain drugs within two hours of driving.
  • Operating a vehicle while impaired and causing bodily harm or death.
  • Refusing or failing to provide a breath, blood or oral fluid sample when lawfully required.

Potential Penalties

Penalties depend on the circumstances, including the level of impairment, whether it is a first or repeat offence and whether harm occurred. Consequences may include:

  • Mandatory fines for first offences.
  • Mandatory jail time for repeat offences.
  • Long-term driving prohibitions.
  • Potential imprisonment for cases involving bodily harm or death.
  • Immediate licence suspensions, vehicle impoundment and ignition-interlock conditions under provincial laws.

A conviction can also create serious immigration problems for permanent residents, temporary residents and applicants. Employment and travel restrictions may also result.

Why You Need a Lawyer

DUI cases involve technical evidence such as breath tests, blood tests, field-sobriety evaluations and police observations. Errors in procedure, improper testing or rights violations may provide strong defences. The way the Crown chooses to proceed, the available disclosure, the timeline of events and the legality of police actions all affect the outcome.

A lawyer protects your Charter rights, challenges unreliable or improperly obtained evidence and guides you through negotiations or trial.
