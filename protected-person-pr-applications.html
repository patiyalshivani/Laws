Protected Person permanent residence guidance

If you have been recognized in Canada as a Protected Person or a Convention Refugee, you may apply for permanent residence inside Canada under the special application class for Protected Persons. This pathway grants you full PR status, enabling you to live, work, study and settle permanently in Canada.

Who Qualifies as a Protected Person?

You qualify if you have been:

  • Recognized by the Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada (IRB) as a Convention Refugee or a Person in Need of Protection.
  • Given a positive decision on a Pre-Removal Risk Assessment (PRRA).
  • Admitted under the Protected Temporary Residents Class.

Are You Eligible to Apply for Permanent Residence?

You are eligible if:

  • You are physically in Canada and hold Protected Person status.
  • You are not a citizen, national or permanent resident of a country where you can safely settle.
  • You are not already a permanent resident of Canada.
  • You are not a “Designated Foreign National” under special immigration rules that bar PR applications for a fixed period.

If you are a Designated Foreign National, you may have to wait a specified number of years before applying for PR.

When Can You Apply?

You may apply any time after receiving notification of your Protected Person status. If you are a Designated Foreign National, you must meet the applicable waiting period before applying.

How to Apply

  • Gather all required application forms and supporting documents.
  • Include proof of your Protected Person status, identity documents, and related family member details.
  • Pay processing fees. Protected Persons are usually exempt from the Right of Permanent Residence Fee.
  • Submit your application via the Permanent Residence Portal or by paper if required.
  • You may be asked for biometrics (fingerprints and photo) after submission.

Family Members and Inclusion

  • You must list all family members, including a spouse or common-law partner and dependent children, whether they are in Canada or abroad.
  • You may choose which family members will be processed for PR under your application.
  • Family members you do not include initially may still be sponsored later, but excluding them may limit future sponsorship rights.
