Canadian citizenship guidance

Becoming a Canadian citizen is a significant step. It gives you the right to vote, apply for a Canadian passport, serve on juries, and fully participate in Canadian civic life. At BG Laws, we guide permanent residents through every stage of this process with clarity, care and professional support.

Eligibility for Adults (Age 18 and Over)

To qualify for Canadian citizenship as an adult, you must meet all of the following:

1. Permanent Resident Status

You must be a permanent resident of Canada, and you must not be:

  • Under a removal order.
  • In the process of losing your PR status.
  • Ineligible due to unresolved residency obligations.

Your PR card can be expired; your status is what matters.

2. Physical Presence Requirement

You must have been physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days (3 years) within the last 5 years before applying. Within those years, at least 730 days must have been spent as a permanent resident.

Some days spent in Canada before becoming a PR may count as half-days, up to a limit.

3. Income Tax Filing

If required under the Income Tax Act, you must have filed taxes for at least 3 years within the last 5 years.

4. Language Ability (Ages 18–54)

Applicants aged 18 to 54 must show ability in English or French for speaking and listening. Approved proof includes language test results, Canadian education transcripts, or equivalent documents.

5. Citizenship Test (Ages 18–54)

You must pass the citizenship knowledge test.

6. No Prohibitions

You may be prohibited from citizenship if you are:

  • Serving a sentence, probation or parole.
  • Charged with or convicted of certain offences.
  • Involved in immigration violations.

Legal review is required if any prohibitions apply.
