Temporary Status & Work Permits

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

The Post-Graduation Work Permit is an open work permit that allows international graduates from eligible Canadian institutions to work for any employer in any industry across Canada. It offers valuable Canadian work experience and helps build eligibility for permanent residence through pathways like the Canadian Experience Class (CEC).

Eligibility Requirements
  • Graduate from a program at a designated learning institution (DLI) and complete it full-time for at least 8 months
  • Apply within 180 days after receiving your final transcript or completion letter
  • Be in valid status (study permit or visitor record) when applying
  • Meet new eligibility rules including language test requirements for programs started November 1, 2024 or later

Validity Period: If your program was 8 months to less than 2 years, your PGWP may match the program length. If your program is 2 years or more, you may qualify for up to a 3-year PGWP.

Documents You’ll Need

Your application should include the supporting documents identified for your program, status, graduation, language results, and other applicable eligibility requirements.

  • Completion letter or final transcript from the DLI confirming you finished your program.
  • Valid study permit or proof of status.
  • Passport, identification, application form, photos, and application fee.
  • Additional documents if your study program deviated from full-time status or had leave.
Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA)

An LMIA is a decision from the Government of Canada that lets an employer hire a foreign worker. It confirms that the job cannot be filled by a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. Employers use the LMIA process when hiring through the Temporary Foreign Worker Program.

What an Employer Must Show
  • The job was advertised to Canadians first.
  • Recruitment efforts were made and documented.
  • The wage and working conditions meet regional and industry standards.
  • The job offer is genuine and full time.
  • All required forms and documents are submitted to Service Canada.
After a Positive LMIA

A foreign worker can apply for a work permit using the LMIA number. The employer must follow program rules during and after hiring. Failure to follow these rules can lead to compliance problems.

High-Wage vs Low-Wage LMIA
High-Wage LMIA

A position is classified as high-wage when the offered hourly wage is at or above the provincial or territorial median wage.

For these applications, employers must submit a transition plan showing how they will reduce reliance on foreign workers over time.

  • Training or hiring Canadian workers
  • Supporting the foreign worker’s path to permanent residence
  • Implementing long-term recruitment strategies

High-wage LMIAs usually apply to skilled or technical positions, supervisory roles, and managerial occupations. Employers may request a work permit up to 3 years for positions paid at or above the provincial median wage.

Low-Wage LMIA

A position is classified as low-wage when the offered hourly wage is below the median wage for that province.

Low-wage LMIAs require additional conditions, such as:

  • A limit on the number of low-wage foreign workers an employer can hire
  • Proof of suitable and affordable housing options
  • Transportation to and from Canada paid by the employer
  • Private health insurance until provincial coverage begins

Low-wage roles often include entry-level, general labour, and support positions. The work permit is usually limited to 1 year. Renewals may be possible, but each renewal requires a new LMIA and is subject to caps and program restrictions.

LMIA-Based Work Permits

An LMIA-based work permit application connects the approved employer position with the foreign worker's work permit application. The employer, offered position, LMIA decision, and supporting documents must be reviewed together.

Spousal Open Work Permit

Spouses or common-law partners of certain foreign nationals in Canada may qualify for an open work permit that allows employment with any eligible employer.

Super Visa

The Super Visa allows parents or grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents to stay in Canada for up to five years at a time, with a visa valid for up to ten years.

Key Requirements
  • Be outside Canada when you apply
  • Pass an Immigration Medical Exam (valid for one year)
  • Hold private Canadian medical insurance (minimum CAD 100,000 coverage)
  • Host must be Canadian citizen/PR and meet Minimum Necessary Income (MNI)
Visitor Visas

Canada's visitor visa system is designed for travel, tourism, or visiting family and friends. The Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) permits entry to Canada for a temporary stay, typically up to six months.

Permanent Residence Programs

Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)

The Provincial Nominee Program allows Canadian provinces and territories to select individuals who have the skills, education, and work experience needed to contribute to their local economy.

How the PNP Works
  1. Determine Eligibility - Each province has its own criteria
  2. Apply to the Province - Submit EOI or apply directly to a provincial stream
  3. Receive Nomination - Province issues certification
  4. Apply for Permanent Residence - Submit through federal system
Express Entry

Express Entry is Canada's main system to select skilled workers for permanent residence. It uses a point-based ranking method.

Programs Under Express Entry:
  • Canadian Experience Class (CEC): For skilled workers with recent Canadian work experience
  • Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSW): For skilled workers with foreign or Canadian experience
  • Federal Skilled Trades Program (FST): For individuals with trade experience
How Express Entry Works:
  1. Confirm Eligibility
  2. Create an Express Entry Profile and receive Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score
  3. Receive an Invitation
  4. Apply for Permanent Residence (within 60 days)
  5. Final Decision
Targeted Express Entry Draws

Canada now conducts specific draws that focus on occupations and skills in shortage, increasing chances of selection for individuals in priority groups.

Priority Groups:
  • Health Occupations
  • STEM Occupations
  • Transport Occupations
  • Trade Occupations
  • Agriculture and Agri-Food Workers
  • French-Language Proficiency

Family Sponsorship

Spousal Sponsorship

As a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or registered Indian who is at least 18 years old, you may sponsor your spouse, common-law partner, or conjugal partner for permanent residence in Canada.

Family Class (Out-of-Canada)

Your partner applies from abroad. After approval, your partner enters Canada as a permanent resident.

In-Canada Class

Your partner lives in Canada. Application processed while they remain in Canada.

Parent and Grandparent Sponsorship

This program allows Canadian citizens or permanent residents to sponsor their parents or grandparents for permanent residence in Canada.

Sponsor Requirements
  • At least 18 years old
  • Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or person registered under the Indian Act
  • Living in Canada when application is submitted
  • Meet or exceed the required income level (Minimum Necessary Income - MNI)
  • Sign undertaking to support parent/grandparent for 20 years (10 years if from Quebec)
Humanitarian and Compassionate (H&C) Applications

The H&C pathway under section 25(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act allows eligible foreign nationals to request permanent residence despite not meeting the usual program requirements.

PR & Citizenship Services

Permanent Resident Card Renewal

A Permanent Resident (PR) card is the main proof of your legal status in Canada. While your PR status does not expire, your PR card does, and renewal is essential.

The 730-Day Residency Obligation

To maintain PR status, you generally must have spent at least 730 days (two years) in Canada during the last five-year period.

Limited exemptions apply for accompanying Canadian-citizen spouses/parents or being employed by Canadian businesses abroad.

Citizenship Applications

Becoming a Canadian citizen gives you the right to vote, apply for a Canadian passport, serve on juries, and participate fully in Canadian civic life.

Eligibility Requirements (Adults 18+):
  • Permanent Resident Status with no removal order
  • Physical Presence: 1,095 days (3 years) in last 5 years
  • Income Tax Filing: For at least 3 years in last 5 years (if required)
  • Language Ability (Ages 18-54): English or French for speaking and listening
  • Pass Citizenship Knowledge Test (Ages 18-54)
  • No Prohibitions: No current sentences, charges or certain convictions

Regional Immigration Programs

Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP)

The Atlantic Immigration Program is a permanent residence pathway for skilled workers and international graduates who want to live and work in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, or Newfoundland and Labrador.

How It Works:
  1. Receive job offer from a designated employer
  2. Employer submits for provincial endorsement
  3. Submit PR application with required documents
Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP)

The Rural Community Immigration Pilot helps skilled workers and international graduates settle in designated rural communities across Canada.

Refugee & Protection Services

Refugee Claims and Appeals

Canada offers refugee protection to people who cannot safely return to their home country. If you face persecution, violence, torture, or other serious harm, you may qualify to make a refugee claim from inside Canada.

You May Qualify as a Refugee or Person in Need of Protection If:
  • Convention Refugee: Well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group
  • Person in Need of Protection: Face danger of torture, risk to life, or cruel or unusual treatment
Protected Person PR Applications

Protected persons may pursue permanent residence applications using the applicable process and supporting documents for their circumstances.

Pre-Removal Risk Assessment (PRRA)

A PRRA is a legal process that allows persons facing removal to apply for protection on the grounds they would face serious risk if returned to their country.

Travel Documents

We assist clients with Refugee Travel Documents and Certificates of Identity for protected persons and those who cannot obtain travel documents from their country of citizenship.

Litigation & Enforcement

When an immigration matter is refused, delayed, or urgent, BG Laws helps clients assess legal remedies and act within strict timelines.

A judicial review challenges an immigration refusal when there is no internal appeal. The Federal Court reviews whether the officer or tribunal acted lawfully, fairly, and reasonably. Deadlines are strict: 15 days for refusals issued inside Canada and 60 days for refusals issued outside Canada.

A stay of removal delays or suspends enforcement of a removal order while a legal matter is pending or where removal may cause serious harm. We prepare urgent evidence, affidavits, legal arguments, and Federal Court materials when required.

Admissibility hearings and detention reviews can affect whether you remain in Canada or in custody. BG Laws prepares identity proof, travel records, personal statements, surety plans, community-tie evidence, and legal arguments for release or defence.

If a sponsored family member's permanent residence application is refused, eligible sponsors may appeal to the Immigration Appeal Division. We prepare the notice of appeal, evidence record, relationship proof, witness preparation, and hearing strategy.

A writ of mandamus asks the Federal Court to compel a government body or official to perform a legal duty, often where an immigration application has been unreasonably delayed. We assess delay, send demand letters, prepare the Federal Court leave application, and monitor follow-up.
