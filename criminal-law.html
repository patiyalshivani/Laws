Bail Hearings and Bail Reviews

When you are charged with a criminal offence in Canada, whether you remain in custody or are released pending trial is critical. Bail affects your liberty, your ability to prepare your defence, and your family and work life.

Bail refers to your release from custody under conditions set by a justice of the peace or judge. Conditions may include reporting requirements, curfews, residence restrictions, no-contact orders, and surety obligations.

A bail review may be available when:
  • New evidence affects the release decision.
  • The circumstances of your case change materially.
  • A legal error or unreasonable decision prevents fair release.

BG Laws responds immediately, prepares release plans, affidavits, surety materials, and bail-review applications, and argues for release with practical conditions.

Impaired Driving and Breath Sample Refusal

Driving while impaired by alcohol, drugs, or a combination of both is a serious criminal offence in Canada. Consequences can affect your freedom, record, immigration status, licence, insurance, employment, and professional future.

What the law covers
  • Operating a vehicle while impaired by alcohol or drugs.
  • Having a blood alcohol concentration of 80 mg or more per 100 ml of blood within two hours of driving.
  • Having prohibited drug levels within two hours of driving.
  • Impaired driving causing bodily harm or death.
  • Refusing or failing to provide a breath, blood, or oral-fluid sample when lawfully required.

DUI cases involve technical evidence such as breath tests, blood tests, field-sobriety evaluations, equipment records, timelines, and police observations. BG Laws reviews procedure, testing reliability, Charter issues, and immigration or professional consequences.

Refusing a breath sample is a separate offence even if you were not impaired. Possible defences include an unlawful demand, unclear instructions, equipment issues, or a medical or physical condition that prevented compliance.

Assault and Sexual Assault

Assault offences cover a wide range of conduct and are taken seriously by police, prosecutors, and courts. A person may be charged where they knowingly apply force without consent, threaten to apply force, or impede another person while openly carrying or using a weapon.

Simple Assault
Often no weapon and no serious injury.
Assault with Weapon / Bodily Harm
More serious penalties where a weapon or actual harm is alleged.
Aggravated Assault
Indictable-only and treated as a very serious non-homicide assault.

Sexual assault requires proof that the accused intentionally touched the complainant, the touching was sexual in nature, and the complainant did not consent. Consent must be voluntary, informed, and ongoing at the time of the touching.

Common defence issues
  • Consent or withdrawal of consent.
  • Honest but mistaken belief in consent based on words or actions.
  • Identity, credibility, inconsistencies, or lack of sexual touching.
  • Accidental contact or lack of intent.
  • Limits on sexual-history evidence and proper court applications where needed.

Criminal Harassment and Uttering Threats

Words, messages, and conduct often form the basis of harassment or uttering-threats allegations. Courts treat these charges as serious matters, and convictions can bring jail exposure, strict bail conditions, and a criminal record.

Criminal harassment may include:
  • Repeated calls, texts, or emails.
  • Showing up at a home, workplace, or school.
  • Following someone in public.
  • Monitoring or watching someone without consent.
Uttering threats may include:
  • Threats of physical harm.
  • Threats of damage to property.
  • Statements implying violence, depending on context.

BG Laws focuses on evidence, credibility, intent, context, and whether the Crown can prove fear, threat, or criminal purpose beyond a reasonable doubt.

Robbery, Extortion, Mischief, Theft, and Fraud

Property and financial charges can create serious risk to liberty, reputation, immigration status, travel, employment, and professional licensing. Early defence action matters.

Robbery involves taking or attempting to take property through violence or threats. Defence strategy often examines force, threats, identity, credibility, Charter breaches, and whether the Crown can prove intent to steal.

Mischief involves intentional interference with property or data, including damage, unsafe use, blocked enjoyment, or computer-data interference. Accidental damage does not meet the legal standard.

Theft requires proof that property belonging to another person was taken without consent with intent to permanently deprive the owner. Issues may include misunderstanding, consent, honest belief of right, identification, and evidence handling.

Fraud involves deceit, falsehoods, or dishonest conduct used to obtain property, money, services, or advantage. BG Laws reviews intent, records, digital evidence, witness credibility, and whether deprivation or risk of loss can be proven.
