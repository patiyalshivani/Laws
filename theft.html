Theft defence guidance

Being charged with theft carries serious legal and personal consequences. Theft is one of the most common criminal charges, but courts treat it as a serious breach of law. A conviction leads to jail time, fines, probation, and a lasting criminal record.

BG Laws provides focused defence for clients charged with theft across Ontario. We build early strategy to protect your rights and future.

What Theft Means

Theft occurs when someone:

  • Takes property belonging to another person.
  • Does so without consent.
  • Intends to permanently deprive the owner of that property.

The Crown must prove all elements beyond reasonable doubt. Intent matters. Accidental taking or honest belief of right often shapes defence.

Common Theft Scenarios

Theft charges arise in many contexts:

  • Shoplifting or retail loss prevention allegations.
  • Theft from employer or workplace.
  • Allegations during disputes with roommates or family.
  • Theft of digital property or electronic data.
  • Theft involving vehicles or tools.

Each case depends on facts, evidence, and witness credibility.

Penalties Upon Conviction

Penalties increase with value and circumstances:

  • Theft under $5,000 carries summary or indictable exposure.
  • Theft over $5,000 carries indictable exposure and higher penalties.
  • Aggravating factors like breach of trust raise sentencing risk.
  • Additional penalties include fines, probation, and restitution orders.

A criminal conviction affects employment, travel, housing, and professional licensing.

Defence Strategy at BG Laws

Defence strategy focuses on key legal issues:

  • Did the Crown prove intent to permanently deprive the owner?
  • Was the property truly owned by another person?
  • Was there a valid excuse, misunderstanding, or consent?
  • Are there errors in identification or evidence handling?
  • Were your Charter rights breached during search or arrest?
