Refugee claims and appeals guidance

Canada offers refugee protection to people who cannot safely return to their home country. If you face persecution, violence, torture, or other serious harm, you may qualify to make a refugee claim from inside Canada.

BG Laws represents refugee claimants at every stage, including claim preparation, eligibility interviews, hearings, appeals, and post-decision steps.

Who Qualifies as a Refugee or “Person in Need of Protection”

Convention Refugee

You have a well-founded fear of persecution based on:

  • Race.
  • Religion.
  • Nationality.
  • Political opinion.
  • Membership in a particular social group.

Person in Need of Protection

You face:

  • Danger of torture.
  • Risk to your life.
  • Risk of cruel or unusual treatment or punishment.

These definitions come from Canada’s immigration and human rights laws. The Immigration and Refugee Board (IRB) decides whether your claim meets these definitions after reviewing your evidence and hearing your case.

Where You Can Make a Refugee Claim

1. Inside Canada (Inland Claim)

If you are already in Canada, you may file a refugee claim online. You submit your forms, identity documents, and a detailed explanation of why you need protection.

2. At a Port of Entry

If you arrive at an airport, land border, or seaport, you may tell the officer that you want refugee protection. The officer will then determine if your claim is eligible to be referred to the IRB.

Stages of the Refugee Claim Process

1. Starting the Claim

You begin your claim online (inland) or at a port of entry.

2. Eligibility Interview

An IRCC or CBSA officer checks your identity, background, and whether the claim is allowed to proceed.

3. Basis of Claim (BOC) Form

You must complete a detailed form explaining:

  • Why you fear returning home.
  • What happened to you.
  • Who harmed you.
  • Why you believe you will be harmed again.
  • Why your government cannot protect you.

You must also submit supporting documents.

4. Preparation for Your Hearing

You prepare evidence including:

  • Police complaints.
  • Medical records.
  • Photographs.
  • Witness letters.
  • Country conditions.
  • Any proof of threats or harm.

Your lawyer prepares you for questioning and presents your legal arguments.

5. Refugee Hearing

The IRB conducts a hearing to assess your credibility, your evidence, and risk in your home country.

6. Decision

The Board may:

  • Accept your claim and recognize you as a Protected Person, after which you can apply for permanent residence.
  • Reject your claim, in which case you may appeal or seek judicial review depending on your situation.

7. Appeal Options

Depending on your case, you may appeal to the Refugee Appeal Division (RAD) or file an application for judicial review in the Federal Court.
