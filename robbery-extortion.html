Robbery defence guidance

A robbery charge places freedom and future at risk. Robbery ranks among the most serious Criminal Code offences. Police and Crown pursue these cases aggressively. Early defence action matters.

BG Laws defends clients charged with robbery, armed robbery, and related offences.

What Robbery Means

Robbery involves taking property from another person through violence or threats. The Crown must prove all elements beyond reasonable doubt:

  • Property was taken or intended to be taken.
  • Force or threats of force occurred.
  • Violence or threats connected directly to the taking.

Robbery differs from theft because violence or intimidation forms part of the allegation.

Common Robbery Allegations

Robbery charges arise in many situations, including:

  • Street or convenience store incidents.
  • Allegations involving weapons.
  • Group or joint enterprise accusations.
  • Disputes escalating into force or threats.

Each case depends on facts, evidence, and witness reliability.

Penalties Upon Conviction

Robbery carries severe consequences:

  • Maximum penalty up to life imprisonment.
  • Higher sentences when weapons are involved.
  • Strict bail conditions during court proceedings.
  • Long-term effects on employment, travel, and status.

Courts impose harsh sentences when violence plays a role.

Defence Strategy at BG Laws

Effective defence focuses on evidence and legal standards. Key issues often include:

  • Whether conduct meets the legal definition of force or threats.
  • Identification errors by witnesses.
  • Credibility and consistency of statements.
  • Charter breaches during arrest or search.
  • Lack of intent to steal.

The Crown must prove every element. Defence challenges gaps and weaknesses.
