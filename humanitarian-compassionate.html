Humanitarian and Compassionate (H&C) guidance

The H&C pathway under section 25(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act allows eligible foreign nationals to request permanent residence despite not meeting the usual program requirements. This special route is used when someone does not qualify under a regular immigration program but would suffer unusual, undeserved or disproportionate hardship if required to leave Canada.

H&C applications are complex, evidence-heavy and deeply personal. Approval depends on presenting a convincing human story supported by strong documentation.

BG Laws prepares detailed H&C applications for individuals and families facing hardship, removal, status loss or other challenges.

1. What Makes H&C Applications Unique

Unlike standard immigration classes where rules are fixed and eligibility criteria are clear, H&C applications hinge on discretion, context, and whole-person assessment.

Key points

  • The focus is not just on meeting criteria but showing that rigid application of the law would result in undue hardship, injustice, or no viable alternative.
  • Decision-makers must assess each case as a whole: your ties to Canada, contributions, hardships, and any vulnerable situations.
  • The eligible relief is extraordinary; it is not intended as a default immigration route.

2. Critical Assessment Factors

A. Establishment in Canada

Your degree of establishment reflects integration. Relevant indicators include:

  • Duration of residence in Canada.
  • Stable employment, education or training.
  • Language proficiency in English or French.
  • Community involvement and volunteer activities.
  • Family and social ties in Canada.

Decision-makers examine how removal would disrupt this established life. The focus is on disruption, not simply accumulation.

B. Hardship on Removal or Outside Canada

Hardship is a central element. Examples include:

  • Serious medical or mental health conditions with inadequate treatment abroad.
  • Family separation or loss of support networks.
  • Unsafe or unstable conditions in the country of origin.
  • Social stigma, discrimination or threats to physical safety.
  • Lack of viable economic or social opportunities.

You must show how these hardships arise because of your specific situation, not general country conditions.

C. Best Interests of Children (BIOC)

When children are affected, their best interests carry substantial weight:

  • Emotional, social, cultural and physical welfare of any child impacted.
  • Disruption to education or living conditions.
  • Country conditions that could impair a child’s well-being.
  • Dependency and attachment to a parent or guardian.

Children’s interests never automatically override all factors, but they can be decisive when compelling.

4. What You Can Request

  • Exemption from specific IRPA or regulation requirements.
  • Permission to apply for permanent residence from within Canada.
  • Consideration of inadmissibility or past status issues on humanitarian grounds, in rare cases.

Process Overview

  1. Engage a lawyer for eligibility assessment and case strategy.
  2. Prepare the application portfolio with narrative, evidence and supporting documents.
  3. Submit your complete application through the proper immigration channel.
  4. Await a decision; processing times can be long and outcomes are unpredictable.
  5. On approval, follow next steps for landing as a permanent resident.
