Rural Community Immigration Pilot guidance

The Rural Community Immigration Pilot is designed to support smaller Canadian communities in attracting skilled workers who intend to settle and contribute locally. The program provides a pathway to permanent residence for eligible foreigners who obtain a job offer in a participating community, meet federal requirements, and receive a community recommendation.

How the RCIP Works

1. Job Offer from Designated Employer

You must secure a full-time, non-seasonal job offer from an employer that is designated under the RCIP in a participating rural community.

2. Community Recommendation

After the job offer, you apply for a community recommendation. The community assesses your job offer, your skills, your intention to live in the community, and your fit with local labour needs.

3. Permanent Residence Application

With the recommendation certificate, you submit your application for permanent residence under federal immigration rules. Some applicants may also apply for a work permit while waiting for their PR decision.

4. Settlement in the Community

The program expects you to live and integrate into the rural community that recommended you, helping fill local labour gaps and grow the local economy.

Eligibility Requirements

To qualify under RCIP, you must meet both federal eligibility criteria and the specific requirements of the community where you apply.

Core Federal Criteria

  • Hold a valid job offer from a designated employer in the community.
  • Intend to live permanently in the community.
  • Demonstrate sufficient language ability in English or French, with the level depending on the job's skill category.
  • Meet the education requirement (a Canadian credential or foreign equivalent).
  • Meet the work-experience requirement, unless exempt, and ensure it aligns with the offered job's NOC/TEER level.
  • If not already working in Canada, prove you have enough funds to support yourself and any family members.

Work Experience Requirement

  • Typically at least 1 year (1,560 hours) of full-time work, or equivalent part-time work, within the last 3 years.
  • Experience must align with the skill level of your job offer.
  • The work may be inside or outside Canada if authorized.
  • Some exemptions apply for recent graduates of designated community institutions.

Education Requirement

  • A Canadian high school diploma or a foreign credential with an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) confirming equivalency.
  • The ECA must be less than five years old at the time of application.

Language Requirement

Your required Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) or Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens (NCLC) level depends on the TEER/NOC skill level of your job offer:

  • TEER 0 or 1: CLB/NCLC 6.
  • TEER 2 or 3: CLB/NCLC 5.
  • TEER 4 or 5: CLB/NCLC 4.

Settlement Funds

If you are not already working legally in Canada, you must demonstrate sufficient funds to support yourself and any accompanying family members until you settle in the community.

Community-Specific Requirements

Each participating community sets its own additional criteria, which may cover local occupation priorities, minimum time living or studying in the community for graduates, specific employer lists or job designations, and community integration commitments.
