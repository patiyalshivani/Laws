Assault charge defence guidance

Assault offences cover a wide range of conduct in Canada and are taken very seriously by law-enforcement, prosecutors and the courts. If you are charged with an assault offence, your freedom, immigration status and reputation may all be at risk.

Legal Definition

Under Canadian law a person commits an assault when they knowingly apply force without the other person’s consent, attempt or threaten to apply force, or while openly carrying or using a weapon impede or accost another person.

Importantly, the victim does not need to suffer a visible injury for an assault charge to stand.

Types and Severity

  • Simple Assault: Often the lowest-level charge. No weapon, no serious injury. It may proceed by summary conviction or by indictment depending on factors.
  • Assault Causing Bodily Harm / Assault with a Weapon: Occurs when a weapon is present or actual bodily harm is inflicted. More serious penalties apply if indictable.
  • Aggravated Assault: The most serious non-homicide assault. It occurs when the victim is wounded, maimed, disfigured or life is endangered. It is an indictable-only offence and carries very high maximum penalties.

Penalties

How an assault charge is tried, summary or indictable, and the severity of harm or weapon involvement significantly affect the possible sentence. For a minor assault the maximum may be up to five years if the Crown proceeds by indictment; for more serious assault it can be up to ten or even fourteen years.

Moreover, a conviction may have immigration consequences, employment consequences, and lasting impact.
