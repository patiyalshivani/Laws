Atlantic Immigration Program guidance

The Atlantic Immigration Program is a permanent residence pathway for skilled workers and international graduates who want to live and work in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, or Newfoundland and Labrador.

The program is designed to help employers in Atlantic Canada fill labour shortages by hiring qualified foreign workers and supporting them in settling long-term in the region.

BG Laws assists employers and workers through every stage of the AIP process.

How the Atlantic Immigration Program Works

1. Job Offer From a Designated Employer

You must first receive a full-time, non-seasonal job offer from an employer who has been formally designated by one of the Atlantic provinces.

2. Provincial Endorsement

Your employer submits your job offer and settlement plan to the province to obtain an endorsement certificate. You cannot apply for permanent residence without this endorsement.

3. Permanent Residence Application

Once endorsed, you submit your PR application with the required documents. Some applicants may also apply for a temporary work permit to begin working in Atlantic Canada while the PR application is processed.

Eligibility Requirements

1. Job Offer Requirements

Your job offer must:

  • Be from a designated employer in an Atlantic province.
  • Be full-time and non-seasonal.
  • Be for a position in an eligible occupation.
  • Include a wage consistent with provincial standards.

2. Work Experience

You generally need:

  • At least one year of full-time or equivalent part-time work experience in the last five years.
  • Experience that matches the job offer.
  • Work completed inside or outside Canada if done legally.

International Graduates Exception

Recent graduates from eligible Atlantic Canadian post-secondary institutions may not need previous work experience.

3. Education Requirements

Your required education depends on the skill level of your job offer.

A. Job Offer in NOC 0 or A / TEER 0 or 1

You must provide one of the following:

  • A completed Canadian one-year post-secondary credential or higher.
  • A completed foreign one-year post-secondary credential or higher, plus an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) showing it is equivalent to a Canadian credential. The ECA must be less than 5 years old when the application is received.

B. Job Offer in NOC B or C / TEER 2, 3, or 4

You must provide one of the following:

  • A completed Canadian high school diploma.
  • A completed foreign high school diploma or equivalent, plus an ECA confirming equivalency. The ECA must be less than 5 years old when the application is received.

4. Language Requirements

Minimum language levels depend on the job:

  • TEER 0, 1, 2, 3: CLB/NCLC 5.
  • TEER 4: CLB/NCLC 4.

Language test results must be valid when applying.

5. Settlement Funds

If you are not already working in Canada, you must show proof of settlement funds. The required amount depends on your family size. Funds must be readily available and unencumbered.

6. Intention to Reside

You must show a genuine intention to live and work in the province that endorses you. Officers assess this through your background, job offer and settlement plan.

Temporary Work Permit Option

AIP allows you to apply for a temporary work permit so you can start working while waiting for your PR decision.

To qualify, you must:

  • Have a job offer from a designated employer.
  • Have applied or be applying for PR.
  • Meet language and education criteria.
  • Obtain a provincial endorsement letter for the work permit.
