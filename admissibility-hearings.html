Admissibility hearings and detention reviews guidance

When you face allegations of inadmissibility to Canada or you are detained under immigration legislation, you may be subject to one of two tribunal processes: an admissibility hearing or a detention review. These are serious legal proceedings with major consequences for your ability to remain in Canada.

What is an Admissibility Hearing?

An admissibility hearing is held by the Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada (IRB) / Immigration Division when the Canada Border Services Agency or other authority refers a report alleging that you are inadmissible under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA). Possible grounds include security, human-rights violations, serious criminality, organized criminality, or failure to meet immigration obligations.

At the hearing the tribunal member reviews the referral, hears evidence from the Minister’s representative and from you, considers your defence, and makes a decision on whether you are inadmissible and what removal order will apply.

What is a Detention Review?

A detention review is a procedural hearing before the IRB / Immigration Division to assess whether your detention under immigration authority should continue or whether you should be released or released under conditions.

When you are detained by the CBSA under immigration law, for example pending removal or admissibility proceedings, you must be afforded a review. Often the first review occurs within 48 hours of detention. If the member orders continued detention, subsequent reviews occur at least every 30 days.

During a detention review the tribunal member considers:

  • The reason for your detention, such as risk of flight, danger to the public, or an inadmissibility ground.
  • Your conduct, status, identity, and travel history.
  • Whether conditions for release, such as reporting, a surety, or residence restrictions, are appropriate.
  • The duration of detention, fairness of the process, and alternatives to detention.
  • The best interests of children if they are affected.

Possible outcomes include release without conditions, release under conditions such as a surety, address, or curfew, or continued detention.

Why These Hearings Matter

  • An admissibility hearing leads directly to a decision on your status. If found inadmissible, a removal order may be issued immediately.
  • A detention review can determine how long you are held, whether you can be released, and whether detention is justified.
  • These hearings often occur under tight timelines with limited opportunity to prepare.
  • Legal representation substantially improves your ability to protect your rights and challenge evidence.
