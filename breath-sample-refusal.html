Breath sample refusal and failure defence guidance

Failing or refusing to cooperate with a lawful demand by a police officer to provide a breath sample is a serious criminal offence in Canada. It is treated with the same gravity as impaired driving. Even if you were not impaired, the offence arises the moment you refuse or fail to meet the demand without a lawful excuse.

What the Law Requires

Under the Criminal Code, an officer may demand that you provide a breath sample if you have operated a vehicle and the officer has reasonable grounds to make the demand. The demand must be lawful, and you must be aware of it. If you knowingly fail or refuse to comply without a reasonable excuse, you commit the offence.

Penalties and Consequences

The penalties for this offence are severe and parallel those for driving over the legal alcohol limit. For example:

  1. A first-offence refusal can result in a minimum fine of $2,000.
  2. A second offence carries a minimum 30 days’ imprisonment and may go up to ten years depending on severity.
  3. Subsequent offences often carry even higher minimums for imprisonment and maximums up to ten years.
  4. Beyond criminal penalties, consequences include driver’s licence suspensions or revocations, vehicle impoundment, increased insurance rates, and significant immigration or professional impacts.

Why You Cannot “Just Refuse”

Many drivers mistakenly believe refusing a breath sample avoids a criminal charge. The law explicitly treats refusal or failure to provide the sample as a separate offence regardless of your blood alcohol content. Even if you believe you are sober, refusing to comply triggers a standalone offence.

Possible Defences

Although the offence is serious, you may have valid defences. These include:

  • The demand was not lawful because the officer lacked reasonable grounds or did not follow proper procedure.
  • You did attempt to provide a sample but were unable due to a medical condition, equipment failure or other reasonable excuse.
  • The warning or instructions given by police were unclear, or the required statutory formalities were not observed.

Your lawyer will review the details: timing, context, officer actions, equipment logs and your medical status.
