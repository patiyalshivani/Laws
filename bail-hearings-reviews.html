Bail hearings and bail reviews guidance

When you are charged with a criminal offence in Canada, the issue of whether you remain in custody or are released pending your trial is critical. Bail and bail review cases affect your liberty, your ability to prepare your defence, and your family and work life.

What is Bail?

Bail refers to your release from custody after being charged with an offence, under conditions set by a justice of peace or judge. The purpose of bail includes:

  • Ensuring you attend your court hearings.
  • Protecting public safety and victims.
  • Maintaining confidence in the justice system.

While all accused persons are presumed innocent, there may be just cause to deny bail if risks cannot be managed.

Conditions may be imposed when you are released. Common conditions include reporting requirements, curfews, residence restrictions, no-contact orders, and surety obligations.

Bail Review: When and How

If bail is denied, or if the conditions of your release are unfair or overly restrictive, you may apply for a bail review. This process allows a higher court to examine whether the original bail decision was flawed.

You may request a bail review when:

  • New evidence affects the release decision.
  • The circumstances of your case change materially.
  • A legal error or unreasonable decision prevents fair release.
