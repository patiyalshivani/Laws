Judicial review guidance

A judicial review lets you challenge an immigration refusal when there is no internal appeal. It is not a new application. It is a legal process where the Federal Court checks whether the officer or tribunal acted lawfully, fairly and reasonably.

The Court does not replace the decision with its own. It reviews how the decision was made and whether the outcome meets legal standards.

When Judicial Review Applies

A judicial review is used for final refusals such as work permits, study permits, visitor visas, permanent residence applications, sponsorship refusals, and other immigration decisions with no further internal remedy.

Deadlines are strict. Fifteen days to file if the refusal was issued inside Canada. Sixty days to file if the refusal came from outside Canada.

Immediate legal advice is essential because missing the deadline usually ends your right to review.

How Judicial Review Works

Stage One: Leave Application

You start by filing an Application for Leave and Judicial Review. This step asks the Court for permission to challenge the refusal. You must explain why the decision is unreasonable or legally incorrect.

Stage Two: Rule 9 Reasons

After filing, the Court requests the officer’s written notes. These notes show how your file was assessed and why the refusal occurred.

Stage Three: Application Record

You then submit your legal arguments. This record explains the errors in the refusal and why the Court should intervene. The Department of Justice replies with its own arguments. You may file a reply if needed.

Stage Four: Decision on Leave

A judge reviews both sides and decides whether the case should move forward. Leave is granted only when the judge sees a serious issue that deserves review.

Stage Five: Hearing

If leave is granted, the Court schedules a hearing. Your lawyer argues that the officer made an error in fact, law or both. The judge later issues a written decision.

If the Court agrees with you, the refusal is cancelled and the application is returned to IRCC for a new assessment by a different officer.

Judicial review does not give automatic approval. It gives you a fair reassessment.

Other Options After a Refusal

A judicial review is not always the best approach. Depending on the refusal, a new application, reconsideration request or another remedy may be faster and more effective. A proper assessment is required before choosing your path.
